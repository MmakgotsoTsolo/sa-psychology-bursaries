How to Write a Funding Motivational Letter

Information about funding motivational letters and the information students may consider including when applying for educational funding.

What Is a Funding Motivational Letter?

A funding motivational letter is a written statement that allows an applicant to provide additional information about themselves, their studies and their reasons for applying for a particular funding opportunity.

The purpose and requirements of a motivational letter can differ between funding providers. Some providers may request a motivational letter as part of the application, while others may provide specific questions or instructions that applicants must follow.

Read the Funding Provider's Requirements

Before writing a motivational letter, students should carefully review the requirements provided by the relevant funding provider.

The provider may specify a word limit, format, topic or particular questions that the applicant must address. These requirements should be followed when preparing the letter.

Introduce Yourself

A motivational letter can begin by briefly introducing the applicant and providing relevant information about their current or intended studies.

The introduction should be relevant to the funding application and provide the reader with a clear understanding of who the applicant is and what they are applying for.

Explain Your Educational Goals

Students may use their motivational letter to explain their educational goals and what they intend to achieve through their studies.

Where relevant, applicants can explain how their current or intended studies relate to their academic interests, future plans or professional aspirations.

Explain Why You Are Applying for Funding

Applicants may explain why they are applying for the particular funding opportunity and how the funding would support their studies.

Where financial circumstances are relevant to the funding provider's requirements, applicants may explain their circumstances accurately and respectfully.

Include Relevant Achievements and Experiences

Where appropriate, students may include relevant academic achievements, leadership experience, community involvement, extracurricular activities or other experiences that relate to the funding provider's selection criteria.

Applicants should focus on information that is relevant to the application and avoid including information simply to make the letter longer.

Keep the Letter Clear and Organised

A motivational letter should be written clearly and organised in a way that allows the reader to understand the applicant's main points.

Students should use complete sentences, appropriate language and paragraphs that connect logically to one another.

Review Your Letter Before Submitting

Before submitting a motivational letter, students should review the content for spelling, grammar, punctuation and accuracy.

Students should also check that the letter follows the funding provider's instructions, including any required word limit, format or specific questions.

Keep Your Information Accurate

Information included in a motivational letter should be truthful and consistent with the information provided elsewhere in the funding application.

Students should not exaggerate achievements, provide false information or claim experiences that they have not had.

Important Information

Funding providers may have different requirements for motivational letters. Some funding opportunities may not require a motivational letter at all, while others may provide specific instructions about what applicants should include.

Always follow the requirements provided by the relevant funding provider when preparing and submitting a motivational letter.

Following the information provided in this article does not guarantee that an application will be successful or that funding will be awarded. Funding decisions are made by the relevant funding provider according to its own eligibility requirements, selection criteria and available funding.
